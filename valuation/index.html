    What actually moves value in LA

    Four things separate the homes that sit from the homes that spark bidding wars — and none of them show up in an algorithm.

    1 · Micro-location

    The right side of a busy street, a quieter block, a better school boundary, a flatter lot. In LA, value can change from one block to the next — Daniel prices the block, not the zip code.

    2 · Condition buyers can feel

    Kitchens and primary baths move money. So does deferred maintenance — buyers discount hard for roof, sewer, and foundation question marks, often more than the repair would cost.

    3 · Space that works

    An ADU, a converted garage done with permits, a real home-office setup, usable flat yard. Flexible space is what LA buyers pay premiums for right now.

    4 · The competition this week

    Your price competes against the homes a buyer tours the same weekend. Daniel checks what's active, pending, and just sold around you before naming a number.

    Six small moves that protect your price

    You don't need a renovation to sell well. These are the high-return basics Daniel walks every seller through — your valuation includes the ones that apply to your home.

    01

    Fix the first 30 seconds

    Curb appeal, a clean entry, working lights. Buyers decide how they feel about a home before they reach the kitchen.

    02

    Handle the scary stuff early

    Roof leaks, sewer issues, unpermitted work — surfacing them early with a plan beats a price cut mid-escrow.

    03

    Declutter to square footage

    Rooms photograph bigger and feel bigger with less in them. You're selling space, not furniture.

    04

    Paint where it counts

    Fresh neutral paint in the main living areas is the cheapest value-per-dollar move in the business.

    05

    Price for the first two weeks

    Your home is worth the most attention the day it hits the market. The right opening price creates urgency — chasing the market down doesn't.

    06

    Get the number before the work

    Request the valuation first. Some "must-do" projects don't move your price at all — find out before you spend.

    Valuation FAQs

    Is the valuation really free?

    Yes — completely. No cost, no obligation to list with Daniel, no pressure call afterward. You'll get your report and a chance to ask questions. If you decide to sell, he'd love to earn the listing; if not, the number is yours to keep.

    How is this different from a Zestimate or Redfin Estimate?

    Those are automated averages built from public records — they can't see your remodel, your lot, or your street. Daniel's valuation is a comparative market analysis (CMA) built by hand from recent closed sales that actually match your home, with adjustments for condition and upgrades.

    Is this an appraisal?

    No. An appraisal is a formal report by a licensed appraiser, usually ordered by a lender. A valuation/CMA is a REALTOR®'s pricing analysis — it's the right tool for deciding what to list for, and it often lands very close to appraised value because it uses the same comparable-sales method.

    Do you need to come inside my home?

    Not for the first valuation — the form plus public records and photos is enough for a solid range. If you're getting serious about listing, a quick walk-through sharpens the number and the prep list.

    How fast will I get it?
